Why NAP Consistency Quietly Makes or Breaks Local Rankings

By Owais Reki

NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone — the three facts Google cross-checks across the web to confirm a business is real and trustworthy. It sounds too simple to matter, but it's one of the most common, quietly damaging issues in local SEO.

Why small mismatches matter

"St." versus "Street," an old phone number still live on one directory, a suite number formatted differently in two places — Google can't tell which version is correct, so it hedges on how much to trust the whole profile rather than picking one.

Build a single source of truth first

Before touching any directory, write down the exact, final version of the business name, address, and phone number. Every listing gets copied from this master record, never retyped from memory.

Audit before you build new listings

Search the business name and city to see what's already out there. Old addresses, listings from a previous owner, or duplicate profiles need fixing before adding anything new — wrong information actively works against the business right now.

Prioritize by authority, not by count

A handful of accurate, high-authority listings — Google, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and one or two industry-specific directories — matter far more than fifty scattered, inconsistent ones.

Consistency is unglamorous work, but it's foundational. Every other local SEO effort performs better once this is solid.
